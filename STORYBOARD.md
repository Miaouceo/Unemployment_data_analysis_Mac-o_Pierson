# Storyboard and demo plan

## The story

**Hook:** Unemployment fell between 2012 and 2023, but did every region benefit equally?

**Context:** Compare 13 metropolitan regions using census unemployment at three dates. The main audience is students and local decision-makers. The rate is different from the ILO rate reported in headline news.

**Analysis:** Begin with the overall rate, follow the three-date trend, then compare the regional map and ranking. Finish with a small 2023 poverty comparison.

**Main takeaway:** A lower overall rate still leaves regional differences. In 2023 the highest and lowest regional rates differ by 4.77 percentage points.

**Implication:** Look at local employment opportunities and living conditions together before recommending action. The dashboard identifies questions for further investigation rather than proving causes.

## Three-minute demonstration

| Time | Show | Explain |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | Title, scope and KPIs | Introduce the question, Insee source and census definition. Mention metropolitan France. |
| 0:25–0:55 | Trend | Show 12.72% in 2012, 13.39% in 2017 and 11.03% in 2023. Explain that there are only three observations. |
| 0:55–1:35 | Map, ranking and year filter | Compare 2012 with 2023. In 2023, point to Hauts-de-France and Pays de la Loire. Hover over the map to show exact rates. |
| 1:35–2:00 | Region filter | Select two regions. Show how the combined KPI and trend change. Explain that the combined rate uses active-population counts. Restore all regions. |
| 2:00–2:35 | Poverty appendix | Explain the fixed 2023 comparison and correlation of about 0.59. Hover over Corse as an exception. Association is not causation. |
| 2:35–3:00 | Quality section and conclusion | Mention complete metropolitan data, the census/ILO distinction and limited observation dates. Conclude with the need to investigate local conditions. |
